# Lore — In-Universe Framing (extracted from mechanics docs)

These are the **"why it makes sense in the fiction"** notes that used to live inside the mechanics
docs. They were pulled out on 2026-06-12 so the mechanics docs describe only *how the system works*
(canon) while the *story wrapped around it* lives here (draft). Each entry names the mechanics doc
it backs; that doc carries a one-line stub linking here.

> None of this is load-bearing. If a framing here changes, the mechanic it describes does **not**
> change — that's the whole point of the split.

---

## Repair Recipes — the "Golden Logic" name
*Backs: [`../economy/economy_alchemy_research.md`](../economy/economy_alchemy_research.md) §4.4*

"Golden Logic" was a misnomer carried over from the early-access lore where pirates thought they
were stealing manufacturing secrets. They were stealing *maintenance* secrets — the great powers'
ability to keep capital ships running indefinitely without burning through Researcher labor was
always the real prize. The name stuck.

## The invisible-hand floor — what it is, lore-wise
*Backs: [`../economy/economy_exchange_pricing.md`](../economy/economy_exchange_pricing.md) §"invisible hand"*

The invisible hand isn't acknowledged in lore at all. In-fiction explanations for "huge orders that
came in and out fast" are left ambiguous — "must have been a Federation Treasury op", "must have
been the Iron Core Reserve managing the float", "must have been some whale". The ambiguity is the
point. The mechanic is the game balancing itself, not a faction acting visibly.

## NPC arbitrage miners — why they're there
*Backs: [`../economy/economy_npc_arbitrage.md`](../economy/economy_npc_arbitrage.md) §"NPC miner owners"*

The three NPC-miner owner types have in-fiction identities (the mechanics doc keeps the mechanical
distinction; this is the flavor):

- **FED mining contractors** — Federation-licensed independent miners selling to FED hubs at the
  local floor price. Lore says these are real people; mechanically they're NPC ships.
- **ICE military extraction crews** — Iron Core's formal military mining ops (Tungsten for railguns,
  Uranium for warheads); armed mining ships with escort patrols.
- **Alliance mining ops** — alliance-owned hubs deploying NPC miners on alliance-controlled belts.

## NPC arbitrage — escort auto-scaling
*Backs: [`../economy/economy_npc_arbitrage.md`](../economy/economy_npc_arbitrage.md) §9.3*

Factions and alliances "notice" repeated losses and assign protection details. Doesn't require any
in-fiction acknowledgment that the system is learning.

## Obligation enforcement — diegetic framing
*Backs: [`../economy/economy_obligations.md`](../economy/economy_obligations.md) §5*

None of the three enforcement tiers is treated as "the game is mad at you." Patrols are "we're
protecting our territory." Tax collectors are "the planet's owner is collecting what's owed."
Default raids are "you defaulted on a national bank loan — the nation's military is collecting." All
three are diegetic, in-fiction enforcement actions, not game-mechanic-shaped abstractions.

## Warlord title — formal registration
*Backs: [`../social/social_war_doctrine.md`](../social/social_war_doctrine.md) §8.2*

In the FED Bureau of Affairs / ICE War Office (or equivalent post-war record-keeping), Warlords are
formally registered. Could tie into faction standing systems (FED awards Warlord with a caveat that
the conquered alliance wasn't NAP'd FED-aligned; ICE awards Warlord with a caveat that the disband
was honorable per war doctrine). Open design space for faction-tinted variants.
